Career tailoring · Ideation

Two CVs, one complete career

Date: 2026-10-19 · Topic: it-mechanical-cv · Context: current CV generator

Focus: Create distinct IT and mechanical-engineering CVs. Add mechanical experience from before 2019, emphasize it for engineering vacancies, and compress recent IT experience.

36 raw ideas → 15 distinct candidates including combinations → 4 ranked survivors. Eleven candidates were cut or absorbed. Four cleared the bar; no fifth was promoted merely to fill the list.

Recommendation: Keep one complete factual master and give it two editorial profiles: IT and Mechanical Engineering. Each profile changes the opening, skill emphasis, and space given to roles. Add the missing engineering evidence before generating the engineering version.

1. One complete factual master, two editorial profiles

Add the pre-2019 mechanical roles to the same complete career record as the IT roles. Maintain two reusable presentation profiles over those facts, rather than two independently edited biographies. The IT profile emphasizes software work; the Mechanical Engineering profile emphasizes verified mechanical work, relevant education, and mechanical skills.

Axis
Career facts and professional positioning
Basis
reasoned: Both CVs describe the same person, so titles, employers, dates, and achievements should have one authoritative home. optimize.md:137,139 already treats the master and role achievements as that authority.
Rationale
New facts are recorded once; differences in emphasis remain intentional and reusable. The first useful content work is to recover each engineering role's actual title, dates, responsibilities, tools, deliverables, and defensible results. Numbers are useful only where known.
Downsides
Requires enriching the master and expressing profile choices. Separate master files are a possible manual trial, but the user would then have to synchronize shared facts; a second file alone does not add a selector to the app.
Confidence
95% — judgment of fit, not a measured outcome
Complexity
Medium

2. Relevance controls detail; dates control chronology

Keep truthful reverse-chronological employment entries while giving relevant mechanical roles most of the explanatory space in the engineering version. Recent IT roles become compact continuity entries, retaining only genuinely useful transferable achievements. This avoids treating “older” as synonymous with “less relevant.”

IT version

Recent IT roles: detailed achievements.
Older mechanical roles: concise coverage.
Skills: software-first.

Engineering version

Recent IT roles: brief dated entries.
Mechanical roles: detailed achievements.
Skills: mechanical-first.

Axis
Engineering emphasis and IT compression
Basis
direct: optimize.md:80–81 says “Current or most relevant role → 4-6 bullets” and “Older or less relevant roles → 1-2 bullets.” Explicit relevance-led allocation resolves this career's overlap.
Rationale
Summary and existing first-page Skills can establish engineering relevance before older roles appear. Inspect the rendered PDF: adding mechanical bullets does not guarantee first-page visibility. IT achievements may support engineering through genuine analysis, automation, or delivery evidence, but must remain attributed to their IT role.
Downsides
Older mechanical roles may still fall later in the document. Title/company/date-only IT entries are a stronger compression variant to discuss; the current prompt expects 1–2 bullets for less-relevant roles. Exact allocations should depend on the evidence available, not force invented achievements.

Complexity

3. Separate professional positioning from employment titles

Let the engineering profile open with an accurate mechanical-engineering identity and a concise explanation of the career trajectory. Keep actual job titles and dates exact in Experience. A professional headline can communicate the engineering background without claiming that the current IT job is a mechanical-engineering position.

Axis
Career facts and professional positioning
Basis
direct: optimize.md:49,53 requires the actual current title first and starts the summary with it. That makes the current IT role set the frame even when the application concerns an earlier engineering career.
Rationale
The two CVs need different introductions as well as different bullet counts. An illustrative positioning direction is “Mechanical engineer with prior engineering experience and a subsequent IT career,” followed by specific, supplied evidence and the target role's relevance.
Downsides
The wording must match the candidate's actual qualifications and experience. Do not imply recent mechanical practice, current CAD proficiency, professional registration, or a combined years-of-engineering total that the source facts do not support. Exact wording awaits the missing history.
Confidence
92%
Complexity
Low

4. Let the application remember its CV type

Choose IT or Mechanical Engineering for an application and retain that choice when regenerating its CV or generating a later cover letter. Make the selected type visible so switching to IT for a different vacancy does not alter the original engineering application's intended emphasis.

Axis
Choosing and preserving a CV type
Basis
direct: tracker.py:187–189,236–238 falls back to the global master when an override is omitted. db.py:347–357 lacks profile identity; server.py:33–38 and extension/popup.js:46–51,82 expose no profile choice.
Rationale
A two-type feature needs the choice to survive the existing application workflow. This is a real code-path gap, not evidence that an existing application has already reverted.
Downsides
Adds selection and persistence work. Saving source snapshots, redesigning interview generation, or building automatic profile detection would broaden the idea beyond the immediate CV/letter need.

90%
