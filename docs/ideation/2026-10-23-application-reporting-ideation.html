Beyond Sankey: where the search is working, and what to change

Focus: dashboard and reporting ideas with data-driven conclusions from the existing dataset, besides Sankey. Seven ranked directions, selected from 39 candidates. This is an idea set, not an implementation plan.

Recommendation: start with the channel decision report, supported by application cohorts and a pipeline-age view. The most useful question is where interview opportunities are coming from, not how many applications were created.

Grounding Context

Read-only aggregate queries against applications.db, plus the status, matching and discovery schemas. The snapshot contains applications created July 1–October 23, 2026. Counts describe recorded activity, not independently verified employer events.

  • Current states, ordered by transitioned_at DESC, id DESC: 126 ghosted, 45 rejected, 10 draft, 4 interview 1, 1 interview 2, 2 withdrawn. One current draft retains an applied event; “currently applied” and “ever applied” are different populations.
  • Fourteen applications reached any interview (7.9% of 178 ever-applied); eight reached round 2 and one reached round 3. No offers or acceptances are recorded.
  • Source coverage: 9 recruiter, 4 referral after case normalization, 174 unrecorded. Recruiter-tagged applications account for 8 of the 14 interview opportunities; unrecorded sources account for 6.
  • Profiles: 184 IT and 3 mechanical engineering. These are too imbalanced for a meaningful profile-performance contest.
  • All 109 application assessments use the same recorded scoring/prompt/model versions. Requirements carry category, priority, evidence level, and evidence paths. Role-family labels need normalization: “frontend” and “Frontend Engineer,” for example, are separate strings.
  • Four mock sessions, none completed or scored; 17 lessons. There is no readiness trend to analyze yet.
  • Ninety discovered postings, zero currently linked applications, 28 with salary data, 80 with known work model, 25 with known seniority. All five discovery runs and all posting sightings are on August 25; this is a historical inventory snapshot.

Key interpretation rule: “ghosted” is a pessimistic default inserted during draft activation, not a measured terminal outcome. tracker.py:249–350 also deletes transient ghosted records and removes history during undo. Use “no recorded outcome” and show age. History is not an immutable audit log.

Eight of fourteen first interviews were recorded within an hour of “applied,” nine on the same calendar day. Timing may reflect backfilling or activation at an advanced stage; it cannot establish real recruiter response speed. Rejection may also be manually recorded without an employer reply. A data-driven conclusion should always show its denominator, observation window, coverage and drilldown.

Verification scope: local queries and targeted source reads. Agent execution was denied, so generation and critique were performed locally without an independent verifier. External research was unavailable because no web-search capability was present. Confidence below concerns a report’s usefulness and feasibility, not statistical certainty about an effect.

1. Channel decision report

Compare observed interview yield by source with horizontal bars or a dot plot, counts beside every rate, and a prominent source-coverage panel. Include age-matched cohorts where possible. A small weekly report could explain which route deserves investigation and link to the underlying applications.

Conclusion supported now: recruiter-tagged entries produced 8/14 recorded interview opportunities. That is worth examining before increasing application volume. The dataset does not show that recruiter outreach causes an 89% success rate: recruiter contact can select for employer interest before tracking begins. “Unrecorded” is not equivalent to cold application.

Strong selection bias, tiny groups, inconsistent source naming, and unknown sources. Show uncertainty and sample sizes; no causal ranking or “optimal allocation” claim.

3. Stage-loss diagnostic

Use a stage conversion table or bars, with separate counts for progressed, recorded rejection, withdrawal and currently unresolved. Split rejections by the highest interview stage recorded before rejection. Unlike Sankey, this report emphasizes denominators and the location of losses.

Conclusion supported now: 37 of 45 recorded rejections have no interview recorded; eight occurred on applications with interviews. Screening is the largest recorded rejection category. Fourteen applications reached an interview and eight reached round 2, but 8/14 is observed progression so far, not a final round-1 pass rate.

Axis
Pipeline progression and age
Basis
direct: application-level status flags and highest recorded interview round. Drafts are excluded from Sankey by sankey.py:23–25.
Rationale
Helps decide whether to investigate targeting/documents or interview preparation; it does not diagnose the cause of a rejection.
Downsides
Skipped/unrecorded stages and active interviews complicate conversion. The largest rejection category need not be the highest-leverage problem.
Confidence
95% · Complexity: Low

4. Targeting evidence audit

Plot match-score bands against observed interview incidence, with a second view by normalized role family. Keep scoring version, candidate version, assessment timing and cohort age visible. This asks whether the scoring system helps choose jobs; it does not interpret scores as probabilities.

Conclusion supported now: five of the six interviewed applications with assessments are in the 80–100 band. This is directionally consistent with useful targeting but involves just six events. A blanket score cutoff is not justified. Unassessed applications must remain a separate group, not receive a zero score.

Axis
Match-based targeting
Basis
direct: 109 assessments with version/hash provenance. matching.py:3–12 defines candidate-to-job fit before tailoring; the tailored CV is not a scoring input.
Rationale
Tests whether a central product signal corresponds to recorded outcomes and helps identify promising niches.
Downsides
Small event count, assessment selection, possible post-outcome scoring, candidate changes and source confounding. Restrict prospective-looking comparisons to assessments recorded before outcomes; normalize role labels before grouping.
Confidence
85% · Complexity: Medium

5. Skill-demand and evidence-gap matrix

Rows are normalized skills or requirement categories; columns show applications requesting them, must-have/core frequency, and evidence level. A Pareto chart can rank recurring gaps, weighted by the jobs actually worth pursuing. Count distinct applications rather than repeated requirements within a JD.

Conclusion supported now: 46 of 109 assessed applications contain at least one technical must-have rated “none”; 50 contain an engineering-practices must-have rated “partial.” These groups overlap. This identifies where to inspect missing or weak CV evidence; it does not prove personal inability or employer rejection reasons.

Axis
Skill evidence
Basis
direct: requirement, category, priority, is_core and evidence_level in matching.py:64–72; aggregate JSON requirement queries.
Rationale
Turns assessments into a focused choice between learning, improving truthful evidence presentation, and changing target roles.
Downsides
LLM labels can be inconsistent; synonyms need normalization, “unknown” must stay separate from “none,” and assessed jobs are a selected sample. Prioritize evidence inspection before prescribing courses.
Confidence
90% · Complexity: Medium

7. Opportunity inventory with freshness and coverage

Use salary range plots by currency, period and comparable employment type, plus work-model/seniority distributions and a feed-health table. Show the inventory date prominently. This is a description of captured postings, not a labor-market benchmark or an application-outcome report.

Conclusion supported now: salary data exists for only 28/90 captured postings (31%), work model for 80/90, seniority for 25/90. All sightings are from August 25. The immediate insight is limited coverage and stale inventory, rather than a trustworthy current market salary.

Axis
Discovered opportunity inventory
Basis
direct: job_postings salary/work-model/seen fields and discovery_runs counters. Five runs: one completed, three completed with errors and one failed. Zero postings are currently linked to applications, despite historical run counters reporting imports.
Rationale
Shows which inventory slices are worth inspecting and why apparent scarcity might reflect collection problems.
Downsides
One stale collection day, missing fields and no current application links. Avoid estimating market trends, feed ROI or salary-versus-interview effects; historical import counters do not establish present lineage.
Confidence
75% · Complexity: Medium
