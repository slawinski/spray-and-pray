Codebase Context

The mechanical profile retains 7 brief IT roles and 5 detailed engineering roles. It already selects only 7 IT achievements from 21. The remaining problem is therefore not simply an unfiltered career record: seven separate role presentations still precede the engineering history.

The writer says “relevance, not recency, determines detail,” but also prescribes a title heading, employer/location/date line and bullet layout for each role. Its “at most 1-2 short source-backed bullets” allowance is per brief role, not a shared IT-era budget. Retained roles may have no selected achievements; source selection also supports omitting roles. These capabilities do not establish support for the new compact or split-section representations below. See selection evidence and writer contract.

The renderer has general role/list spacing, no compact brief-role mode, and no role-detail input. Its fixed section mapping also means two differently labeled Experience sections can collide rather than render independently. This makes #3 a substantive structural proposal, not a heading-only edit. See layout and section parsing.

Observed baseline, with limits. The inspected source/layout preview has three pages, with engineering beginning on page 2. It prints approved source selections directly; it is not tailored model output or a checker-approved application. No survivor has demonstrated height savings, a new page count, first-page engineering placement, or ATS performance.

Every retained employment remains distinct, with its exact historical title, employer, actual dates and applicable location facts. Any achievement belongs to its own source role. A compact era label is not a composite job or evidence of gap-free employment. Older engineering work must never appear current.

Evidence scope: three source dossiers and a fresh critique of all 16 candidates informed this comparison. Five inherited-model generators covered six frames; the generator covering two frames shared one verification cap, so idea-level scrutiny was not uniform. External research was unavailable because no web-search tool was available; no external research claim underpins these recommendations.

Topic Axes

  • Source selection: which roles and achievements earn space in a mechanical application.
  • Representation: how truthful employment facts are grouped, attributed and ordered for reading.
  • Layout: how readable spacing and actual render position affect the IT passage.

All three axes have survivors: selection (#2, #4, #5), representation (#1, #3), and layout (#6, #7). These are alternative directions and compatible supporting choices, not seven features to build together.

1. C16 — Compact IT work-history ledger with one shared achievement budget

Axis
Representation; supports selection
Complexity
Medium
Confidence
88%
Description
Keep all seven distinct IT employments, but replace seven miniature heading/metadata/bullet sections with compact dated records. Give the entire IT era one small allowance of short, role-attributed achievements instead of an allowance for every employer. One or two SHORT achievements across the whole era is an illustrative editorial target, not existing behavior or a binding specification. A record with no achievement remains visible; any retained clause sits with its exact source appointment.
Basis
direct: the writer prescribes “Per role, use this exact layout” and allows “at most 1-2 short source-backed bullets” per brief role (writer contract). reasoned: repeated identity scaffolding and repeated opportunities to elaborate are separate space costs. A compact ledger plus a shared allowance addresses both; sentence trimming alone addresses neither completely.
Rationale
This is the strongest combined answer to structure and prose while retaining the full employment record. Its shared allowance can be allocated using #4, and the chronological edition can keep IT before engineering unless #3 is chosen separately.
Downside
This is a new writer/renderer representation, not an existing ledger capability. Long titles and metadata still wrap: seven records do not mean seven physical lines. Attribution and readable boundaries must survive compaction, including separate appointments at the same employer. The allowance cannot promise a page result.

2. C07 — Title/company/dates only for every IT role

Axis
Source selection
Complexity
Low
Confidence
91%
Description
Retain all seven IT employment identities and select no IT achievements. Keep exact titles, companies, dates and applicable locations; let the detailed engineering roles carry the achievement narrative. “Title/company/dates only” is shorthand for complete role identity without achievement prose.
Basis
direct: the resolver accepts empty achievement selections while appending retained identities (selection evidence). The writer explicitly says an empty array means “title/date-only, not permission to fabricate or omit the role” (writer contract).
Rationale
The easiest strong reduction: a clear editorial choice removes all selected IT achievement prose through an existing selection mechanism. Its low effort and direct relevance justify second place even though it changes less structure than #1.
Downside
Useful transferable evidence disappears too. Under the current format all seven role headers and metadata blocks remain, limiting structural gain. Empty-selector support is not a measured end-to-end layout result.

3. C15 — Engineering experience first; compact recent IT history second

Axis
Representation
Complexity
High
Confidence
84%
Description
Present a clearly labeled Engineering Experience section before a compact Recent IT Employment History section. Keep reverse chronology within each section and retain exact historical titles and truthful dates. This explicitly departs from today's global reverse-chronology rule. Engineering remains visibly older; relevance order must never imply recent engineering practice.
Basis
direct: the current writer says “Never reorder roles for any reason” (chronology contract), and the renderer normalizes Experience-like headings to one canonical section (section parsing). reasoned: showing engineering before recent IT changes the first-read narrative directly, whereas merely shortening IT only makes engineering potentially nearer.
Rationale
The most direct narrative improvement. Combined with #1 it offers both an engineering-led reading order and genuine IT compression. Reordering and compressing are distinct mechanisms: only the compact IT representation reduces the IT block.
Downside
Higher structural cost: current heading normalization can overwrite one Experience section with another, while unrecognized headings can go unrendered. Writer, checker and localized section conventions currently assume the existing structure. Clear dates are essential, and neither a first-page result nor universal reader/ATS preference is established.

4. C01 — Keep only distinct transferable IT evidence

Axis
Source selection
Complexity
Medium
Confidence
82%
Description
Retain IT achievement prose only where it adds a distinct signal relevant to the vacancy. One or two achievements about data analysis, automation or test tools might earn space, but only if the actual source role supports those facts. Generic frontend-stack prose does not become mechanical evidence through relabeling. Keep the remaining roles as identities rather than awarding every employer a bullet.
Basis
direct: detail is governed by relevance, and an achievement must derive from its own role's source achievements (writer contract; factual attribution). reasoned: marginal usefulness to the vacancy is a better allocation principle than equal prose per employer. Seven selected claims do not prove either duplication or distinct value.
Rationale
Removes repetition where it actually exists and preserves a reason for including IT evidence. This is a useful selection principle for #1's shared budget, rather than an excuse to invent engineering accomplishments from IT employment.
Downside
Selection needs the vacancy and the actual approved facts. Similar themes can still convey different responsibilities; excessive trimming can erase useful context. All seven identity scaffolds remain unless paired with a structural choice.

5. C02 — Transparently selected IT employment history

Axis
Source selection
Complexity
Medium
Confidence
76%
Description
Omit selected IT employments from the mechanical projection and label the history transparently as selected, with no claim to be exhaustive. Preserve each displayed role's exact identity and dates. Retain enough recent context to explain the career transition where supported, rather than treating the entire recent career as disposable.
Basis
direct: profile membership already distinguishes retained and omitted roles (selection evidence). reasoned: removing a role removes both its prose and its identity scaffold, making this the highest content-compression option, at the cost of completeness.
Rationale
Offers a genuinely different tradeoff from the all-seven-role options: relevance can win over a complete employment register, provided the document is honest about its scope.
Downside
Omissions can require explanation and may be inappropriate where an application requests full history. A selected-history heading must be meaningfully displayed under the section contract. Do not merge dates, imply an uninterrupted employment span without retained evidence, or suggest that omitted roles never happened. No particular subset or saving is established.

6. C05 — Profile-scoped compact IT spacing at readable type

Axis
Layout
Complexity
Medium
Confidence
65%
Description
Give the mechanical profile's brief IT entries tighter, readable role and list spacing while retaining the fuller rhythm of detailed engineering entries. This can preserve every selected word and identity if desired. Compaction is about repeated gaps and role scaffolding, not shrinking fonts as the primary answer.
Basis
direct: current main text is 10.5pt, role headings have a 15px top margin and lists a 15px top margin, with no role-specific compact styling. Brief/detail rules exist separately but are not input to the renderer (layout evidence; detail boundary).
Rationale
A distinct preserve-content alternative when deleting useful evidence is unattractive. It may also support #1, although the shared content budget and compact records are the stronger primary mechanisms.
Downside
Lower confidence until actual rendering is measured. Nominal CSS margins do not add up to proven savings. Crowded boundaries hurt scanning; keeping a large block together can leave page-bottom gaps. It needs correct role-scoped metadata and does not establish where engineering will begin.

7. C09 — Advisory render-preview target for the engineering start

Axis

Complexity
Low

Description
Use a desired engineering-start position—such as the first page—as an advisory reference when inspecting the actual tailored render. Show where engineering really begins and warn when the chosen direction misses that preference. This narrowed idea is a preview aid, not a new blocking gate or an automatic regeneration loop.
Basis
direct: pagination continues until content is exhausted, with no physical fit target (pagination evidence). reasoned: observable placement is a better check on “brief enough” than word counts or model-token allowances, which cannot establish physical fit.
Rationale
Supports the other choices by exposing the result that matters to reading order. An honest missed target is useful feedback; it does not authorize silently omitting roles or inventing tighter facts.
Downside
Not a standalone content-compression mechanism. A first-page target is a preference, not an evidenced requirement or proven outcome. Actual output may miss it even after reasonable compaction, and preview inspection adds effort. Low complexity applies to this advisory comparison, not to the rejected automated adjustment loop.

How the choices combine

#1 + #3 is the recommended combination for an engineering-led narrative: #1 compresses the IT structure and prose; #3 changes which dated experience the reader encounters first. #4 can decide which achievements deserve #1's small shared allowance. #6 is optional physical support, and #7 supplies advisory feedback about actual placement.

#2 is the quickest path. It removes every IT achievement while retaining the complete timeline. It is an alternative to retaining selected IT achievements under #1 or #4, not an additional achievement allowance. #5 is the separate completeness tradeoff when transparent omissions are acceptable.
